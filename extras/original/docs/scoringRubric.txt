   The question is:
   \"Is the assigned phenotype label clinically plausible and internally

3) Evidence types must be explicitly categorized.

   Evidence rules must use the following categories:
   - peri_index
   - post_index
   - treatment
   - utilization
   - complications

   This is the SINGLE COMPREHENSIVE rubric that will be used for automated
   phenotype validation.

   Generate multiple independent evidence rules within EACH clinically applicable

   Because there is only ONE rubric, it must contain broad clinical coverage and
   substantially more rules than would be appropriate for one member of a
   multi-rubric ensemble.

   Prefer corroborating signals such as:
   - disease-specific procedures
   - disease-specific medications or supplies
   - medication initiation
   - medication persistence or refill continuity
   - combination therapy
   - treatment escalation or step-up therapy
   - peri-index management
   - post-index continuity of care

   - disease-relevant utilization
   - disease-consistent complications and their management

   Do not artificially limit the number of evidence rules for brevity.

   Include additional rules whenever they capture clinically distinct and
   independently evaluable evidence.


4) Single-signal validation is NOT allowed.

   A YES decision must require evidence from at least TWO DISTINCT evidence
   categories.

   Multiple rules from the SAME category do NOT satisfy this requirement.

   Examples:
   - Three medication/treatment rules alone cannot establish YES.
   - Two utilization/procedure rules alone cannot establish YES.
   - Medication + utilization may satisfy the cross-category requirement if the
     overall scoring threshold and all other constraints are also satisfied.

   Whenever clinically appropriate, favor YES decisions supported by more than
   two independent signals, while retaining the minimum requirement of two
   distinct categories.

   Closely related manifestations of the same underlying event should not be
   treated as independent corroboration merely because they are represented by
   different administrative codes.


5) Exclusion criteria MUST be explicitly modeled and evaluated BEFORE scoring.

   Exclusions are NOT negative evidence.

   There are two exclusion classes:

   A) HARD EXCLUSIONS:

      - Alternative situations or contexts that invalidate the assigned label or
        make reliable validation impossible.

      - If triggered, the decision must be NO or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.

      - Hard exclusions should be used sparingly and should require high-confidence
        evidence.

      - Do not use a hard exclusion merely because another diagnosis is possible.


   B) ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS:

      - Strong competing diagnoses, competing etiologies, alternative treatment
        indications, and rule-out patterns.

      - These apply penalties and/or cap the maximum possible decision.

      - Generate an EXTENSIVE and COMPREHENSIVE set of alternative-explanation
        rules.

      - Do NOT restrict this section to only the most common two or three
        differential diagnoses.

      - Consider all major clinically plausible differential diagnoses that could
        produce the observed claims pattern.

      - Consider alternative indications for medications, procedures, utilization
        patterns, and complication patterns used as positive evidence elsewhere
        in the rubric.

      - When clinically appropriate, create SEPARATE rules for distinct competing
        explanations rather than combining many unrelated differential diagnoses
        into one broad rule.

      - Include the diagnosis, treatment, procedure, utilization, or temporal
        pattern that makes each differential diagnosis a credible competing
        explanation.

      - Use large penalties and/or decision caps for highly specific competing
        patterns.

      - Differential-diagnosis rules should be strict enough that merely having a
        diagnosis code for a possible alternative condition does NOT automatically
        negate otherwise coherent phenotype evidence.

      - A large penalty should generally require a coherent competing pattern,
        not merely an isolated competing diagnosis code.


6) Evaluation order MUST be:

   (1) Hard exclusions
   (2) Alternative explanations
   (3) Positive evidence scoring
   (4) Cross-category requirements
   (5) Decision thresholds and decision caps
